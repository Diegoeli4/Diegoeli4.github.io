Variation Across Contexts

Course outcome: identify, analyze, and reflect on how writing and language change from one context to another (cultural, digital, workplace, and academic), including in my own writing.

What This Outcome Means to Me

To me, variation across contexts means that the way people write and talk changes depending on where they are, who they are talking to, and what they are trying to do. Every group has its own rules for communicating. A text to a friend, an essay for a professor, and a message to teammates in a video game all follow different patterns, and something that makes sense in one place might be confusing or even wrong in another.

Artifact 1: Analysis of Literacy Practices of League of Legends

Download the full analysis (Word document)

Description: An analysis of the League of Legends player community as a discourse community, looking at the specialized words players use in game and the tools, like Mobalytics, that they create to understand the game.

Context

I wrote this analysis for ENC1101 with Professor Benton. The assignment asked me to study the literacy practices of a community I am part of. I chose League of Legends, a 5v5 online game that has been popular since 2009. My audience was my instructor and classmates, many of whom do not play the game, so I had to explain terms that players use without thinking. I used Dan Melzer's definition of a discourse community, a group that shares goals, ways of communicating, genres, and a lexis, to support my analysis.

Reflection

This artifact shows how much language changes from one context to another. Inside the game, players use short terms like "gank," "CS," "wave freezing," "top diff," and "inter," and they also communicate with pings like "Enemy missing." These words are fast and efficient for players, but they mean almost nothing to someone outside the community. Writing this analysis, I had to translate that language into academic writing by defining each term and explaining why it exists. I realized that a lot of the language I use every day follows rules I never thought about, and that "good" communication depends on the context. A message like "be careful, jungler might gank top" is clear and effective in the game, but I could not write that way in an essay without explaining it.

Connection to Writing and Lived Experience

Moving between gaming language and academic language is similar to how I move between Portuguese and English and between home, school, and online spaces. In each place I adjust my words to fit who I am talking to. In the game, knowing the lingo gives you a place in the group, and players who don't know it get called a "noob." In class, the expectations were different: I needed a clear structure, quotes, and citations. Writing for an audience that did not know the game pushed me to slow down and explain things I usually take for granted.

Transfer

In computer science and cybersecurity, there is a lot of specialized vocabulary, just like in League of Legends. In the future, before I write something, I will ask who my audience is and whether they share my vocabulary. If I am writing for other programmers, technical terms make the writing faster and clearer. If I am writing a report or explaining a security problem to people who are not technical, I will need to define terms and explain ideas the way I did in this analysis.
