Writing & Power

Course outcome: critically examine and act on the relationship among identity, literacy, language, and power.

What This Outcome Means to Me

To me, writing and power means that the way people use language is connected to who has control and who gets respected. Schools, governments, and other institutions decide what kind of writing counts as "correct," and those decisions affect how people learn and how they are judged. People who speak more than one language or don't write in the expected way can be seen as less capable, even when their language experience is actually a strength.

Artifact 1: Reading Journal 4

Download Reading Journal 4 (Word document)

Description: A reading journal where I chose quotes from two course readings and responded to each one. The first reading is Barger's "Reading Is Not Essential to Writing Instruction," and the second is about multilingual writers and "rhetorical attunement."

Context

I wrote this reading journal for ENC1101 with Professor Benton. The format was a two-column table: on one side a quote from the reading, and on the other side my response. My audience was my instructor, and the purpose was to show that I understood the readings and could connect them to my own experiences. Because it was a journal and not an essay, my responses were shorter and more informal.

Reflection

Both readings showed me how power shapes writing. Barger explains how No Child Left Behind created a lot of testing and made teachers worried about losing their jobs over low scores. I responded that, from my own experience in school, some teachers seemed more focused on test scores than on helping students become deep readers. I also noticed that tests full of grammar and vocabulary questions can leave students without real writing skills. Before this class, I saw those tests as normal, but now I see them as decisions made by institutions that control what students learn. The second reading showed multilingual writers like Alicia and Yolanda using their languages as strengths. Yolanda and her classmates in Colombia had to translate English science articles into Spanish because translations were not available, which shows how English holds power in academic fields. It also shows how multilingual people find ways to work around that.

Connection to Writing and Lived Experience

The idea of rhetorical attunement connects to my own life. I was born in São Paulo, Brazil, and moved to Florida when I was six, so I grew up using both Portuguese and English. Like the writers in the reading, my experience with more than one language helps me notice differences in how people communicate. Reading about Alicia learning Portuguese from books bought in Brazil, and about bilingual teachers using language play in their classrooms, made me see my multilingualism as something that gives me an advantage instead of something I need to hide. I also went through the same school system that Barger criticizes, so the reading helped me understand my own education differently.

Transfer

Because of these readings, I will pay more attention to who decides the rules of writing and why. In my future classes and career in computer science and cybersecurity, I will work with people from many language backgrounds, and a lot of technical knowledge is written only in English. I want to remember Yolanda's example and make my own writing, such as documentation and reports, clear enough that people who are still learning English can understand it. I will also try to see my teammates' multilingualism as a strength, the same way I now see my own.
